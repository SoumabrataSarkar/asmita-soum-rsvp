How do I get to Barrackpore from the airport or the station?

We are thrilled you are coming. Here are your options for reaching us in Barrackpore.

From the airport · Dum Dum

A prepaid cab, Rapido or Uber takes roughly 30–40 minutes to the venue and the accommodation.

From Howrah Station

A taxi takes roughly 1 to 1½ hours depending on traffic, and is the most straightforward route. You can also take the metro to Sealdah and a local train from there.

From Sealdah Station · the local train

Scenic and very cheap. A direct local train from Sealdah to Barrackpore takes about 40 minutes, and they run every 10–15 minutes.

The river route from Howrah · for the adventurous

If you want something memorable: a direct train from Howrah to Serampore takes 21–30 minutes. From Serampore, catch the ferry across the Hooghly to Dhupighat in Barrackpore, about 15 minutes. Scenic, historic, and it feels like stepping into Bengal’s colonial past. A short cab from the ferry brings you to the venue.

Do reach out if you would like help planning any of these.

What is the weather like in late January?

Late January is one of the best times to be in Kolkata.

  • Temperature — 15 to 25°C. Cool mornings and evenings, warm afternoons.
  • Weather — clear skies, a little morning mist, rain very unlikely.
  • Humidity — very low.
  • What to pack — light layers, a shawl or cardigan, comfortable shoes for walking, sunglasses, sunscreen.

Genuinely one of the city’s most beautiful seasons, and perfect for exploring.

When should I arrive?

If you are staying with us, accommodation is available from about 12:00 PM on 26th January, so plan your arrival accordingly.

That said, we would strongly suggest coming a day or two earlier if you can. It gives you time to see Kolkata at its best and to settle in without rushing before the Sangeet at 6:30 PM on the 26th.

Kolkata in late January is magical — give yourself a day or two to wander, eat and take it in before the celebrations start.

Is there parking at the venue and the accommodation?

Yes, at both. Nothing to worry about — just tell us when you are arriving and what you are driving, and we will keep a spot for you.

What if I have a food allergy?

There will be a generous spread of Indian and Bengali food across the celebrations, vegetarian and non-vegetarian both.

Please note that the menu will include:

  • Seafood and fish
  • Meat dishes
  • Nuts, in various preparations and in the sweets

If you have any allergy or dietary restriction, tell us in advance and we will make arrangements for you. Your health and comfort come first.

What should I do with two days in Kolkata?

Kolkata is best taken slowly — old architecture, neighbourhoods, food, and long walks with plenty of chai. Here is a two-day route that covers the best of it without racing from one end of the city to the other.

Day one · Colonial Kolkata, the Maidan and the Hooghly

Start at the Victoria Memorial. Walk the gardens, and go into the museum if history interests you.

Then St Paul’s Cathedral nearby, for the Gothic architecture and the stained glass. If you like heritage buildings, St John’s Church fits in later while you are in old Kolkata.

Cross the Maidan, the city’s enormous green heart, and take in ordinary Kolkata life — cricket, football, chai stalls, people simply sitting about.

Lunch on Park Street. Peter Cat is the classic, and the Chelo Kebab is worth ordering. Flurys is the other option, for an old-school café afternoon.

Wander Park Street and the heritage buildings around it, then head to Prinsep Ghat in the late afternoon. This is where you should slow down: sit by the Hooghly, get a chai, walk along the water, or take a boat out around sunset for the view of Vidyasagar Setu.

Dinner at 6 Ballygunge Place or Bhuter Raja Dilo Bor for Bengali food — or back to Park Street for a livelier evening. Olypub is an institution.

Day two · Old Kolkata, Kumartuli and street food

Start with a proper Bengali breakfast — luchi or kochuri with aloor dom, then mishti doi or a few sweets.

Spend the morning in North Kolkata: the old lanes, the heritage houses, the neighbourhoods that still feel like old Calcutta. Go to Kumartuli, where the artisans build the city’s Durga idols out of straw and clay.

Or, instead, Jorasanko Thakur Bari — Rabindranath Tagore’s ancestral home. Plenty of history either way.

Then College Street, one of the largest second-hand book markets in the world. Stop at the Indian Coffee House if the literary side of the city interests you.

Lunch should be a full Bengali meal — bhetki paturi, shorshe fish, chingri malai curry, aloo posto, dal, depending on what is going that day.

In the afternoon, New Market for the classic Kolkata market experience: clothes, jewellery, leather, and a great deal of food. Later, Chinatown, for the old Chinese quarter and its particular Indo-Chinese cooking.

Finish with a food crawl. You should not leave without:

  • Phuchka
  • An egg or chicken roll
  • Momos
  • Mishti and mishti doi — Balaram Mullick, if you can
  • Roadside chai in a kulhad, as much as you can manage

If you have extra time

If temples matter to you, Kalighat is the quintessential Kolkata one. Dakshineswar and Belur Math pair well as a half-day of their own, but are best left out of a two-day trip unless they are high on your list.

A tram ride is a lovely bit of old Kolkata if you happen on a route that suits you — but do not build your day around it.

The best of Kolkata is not in getting through a list. Leave room to wander, sit by the river, get lost in the lanes, stop for chai, and eat far more than you meant to.

OUR STORY
Asmita & Soumabrata

The Beginning

We've known each other for 20 years. But here's the thing — we met as 9 year olds and were only in school together for 4 years. Four years. After that, life pulled us in different directions and we never actually imagined this is how our story is going to turn out. Back then, we were just two kids sharing auto rides and exchanging dictionaries. But little did we know.

Staying Connected

What kept us tethered through the distance was the weirdest thing: birthday calls. At first, we'd beg our parents for the phone on each other's birthdays. Just a quick call as a ritual. Then we moved to college and got our own phones, and the ritual stuck. Almost every single birthday, a call would come through. For 13 years, that's how we marked time.

A Pattern of Missing

Meanwhile, life kept pulling us apart. We'd end up in the same cities — Kolkata, Pune, Bangalore — but never at the right moment. Thirteen years of missing each other by days, months, sometimes years. Always close, never quite there.

December 15th, 2023

BANGALORE

Finally she came to attend a friend's wedding and he was living in the same city at the same time. We stayed up the whole night under the stars — jamming, talking about agritech, throwing around startup ideas, having conversations where time just disappears. And just like that when the sun came up, something shifted. We looked at each other and we both realized we weren't “just friends” anymore.

Three Years Later

Midnight sushi runs, monsoon treks, flights booked whenever we could afford them, bike rides through cities, Google Meet dates across time zones with a bowl of ramen, celebrating each other's small and big wins, impromptu trips that made no sense but felt like everything. We just kept showing up.

The Sun and The Moon

She's scared of water but she's learning how to surf with him. He's a nightowl but wakes up at 6:30 AM for runs for her. We're nothing alike. We're different and codependent as the sun and the moon. And now, after 20 years of being clueless about how life could actually take such a surreal turn like this, we're getting married!

— and you are part of what comes next.
